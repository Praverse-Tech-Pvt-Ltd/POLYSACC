The Core Insight

Four conditions — IC/BPS, Diabetic Nephropathy, Osteoarthritis, and Hyperlipidaemia — are clinically distinct in their presentation, yet share a single molecular thread: the progressive disruption of polysaccharide-based protective surfaces. In the bladder, it is the glycosaminoglycan (GAG) layer. In the kidney glomerulus, heparan sulphate proteoglycans. In articular cartilage, chondroitin and keratan sulphate. On the vascular endothelium, heparan sulphate chains that govern lipoprotein clearance.

Polysaccharide Chemistry Pvt Ltd has built its pharmaceutical portfolio on this same molecular family. The biochip initiative is a natural extension of that expertise — a point-of-care biosensor platform capable of detecting polysaccharide-layer biomarkers across multiple chronic disease states, enabling early flare detection, treatment response monitoring, and companion diagnostics for Polysacc's therapeutic portfolio.

"The bladder, the kidney, the joint, the vessel wall — four organs, four diseases, one molecular failure: the polysaccharide layer breaks down. We already make the medicine. Now we build the sensor."

The Four Indication Framework
01 IC / BPS · Bladder

Interstitial Cystitis /
Bladder Pain Syndrome

The GAG layer lining the bladder urothelium is degraded — allowing urinary solutes to penetrate the epithelium and trigger chronic neurogenic pain, urgency, and frequency. IC/BPS remains a diagnosis of exclusion, with a median 5–7 year journey from symptom onset to correct diagnosis. A biochip measuring urinary GAG fragments, APF (antiproliferative factor), and urothelial permeability markers would close this diagnostic gap — and identify patients most likely to benefit from Pentosan Polysulfate Sodium therapy.

Urinary GAG APF HB-EGF GP51
02 DN · Kidney

Diabetic
Nephropathy

The glomerular basement membrane is protected by heparan sulphate proteoglycans — the same molecular class as bladder GAG. In diabetic nephropathy, hyperglycaemia degrades these chains, increasing glomerular permeability and driving proteinuria. Early detection of heparan sulphate fragments in urine predicts DN progression before clinical proteinuria appears. The biochip creates a window for intervention — and a companion test for heparin-analogue nephroprotective therapies, including PPS's anti-inflammatory properties.

Heparan Sulphate fragments uACR TGF-β1
03 OA · Cartilage

Osteo-
arthritis

Articular cartilage integrity depends on a matrix of chondroitin sulphate, keratan sulphate, and hyaluronic acid — all polysaccharides. OA begins silently: years before X-ray changes, cartilage GAG is leaching into synovial fluid and blood. A biochip that quantifies COMP (cartilage oligomeric matrix protein) and urinary CTX-II (collagen type II breakdown) enables pre-radiographic staging, flare monitoring, and response tracking for polysaccharide-based chondroprotective interventions — an area directly aligned with Polysacc's chemistry competency.

COMP CTX-II Chondroitin-S fragments
04 HL · Vasculature

Hyper-
lipidaemia

Endothelial heparan sulphate proteoglycans are the primary binding sites for lipoprotein lipase and LDL receptor-related proteins — the gatekeepers of triglyceride and LDL clearance. When this glycocalyx is damaged — by oxidative stress, hyperglycaemia, or chronic inflammation — lipid accumulation accelerates independently of dietary input. Polysulphated polysaccharides including PPS have demonstrated heparin-like lipid-lowering activity. A biochip detecting endothelial glycocalyx degradation products links the vascular and metabolic dimensions of Polysacc's portfolio.

Syndecan-1 HS fragments Lp(a) ApoB

Why Polysacc — Why Now

Molecular expertise. Polysacc's core competency is polysaccharide chemistry — the same molecular family that underpins every biomarker in this platform. No other Indian pharmaceutical company is positioned to develop, own, and commercialise a polysaccharide biosensor with this level of scientific coherence.

Portfolio synergy. Each biochip indication has a direct pharmaceutical companion in the Polysacc portfolio: PPS for IC/BPS and DN, polysulphated polysaccharides for HL, and chondroprotective chemistry for OA. The biochip is not a standalone device — it is a companion diagnostic that identifies the right patient for the right Polysacc molecule.

Unmet diagnostic need. All four conditions share a diagnostic failure mode: late detection. IC/BPS takes 5–7 years to name. DN is typically caught after established proteinuria. OA is staged radiographically, not biochemically. HL is monitored by lipid panels that miss glycocalyx dysfunction entirely. The biochip fills gaps that current laboratory infrastructure cannot address.

Platform scalability. The microfluidic architecture is modular — a core GAG-detection chip with indication-specific biomarker panels as add-on modules. Initial development focuses on IC/BPS (highest clinical urgency; PPS companion diagnostic), with DN, OA, and HL as staged expansions.
